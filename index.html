The Opportunity

The RTD Category at Campus Venues Is Wide Open.

The SEC lifted its stadium alcohol ban in 2019. By 2024, all 16 SEC schools sell alcohol to the general public. The NCAA cleared hard seltzer specifically for stadium sales in March 2023. Texas A&M generated $2.4 million in gross alcohol revenue in their first year of general sales.

Beer gets the shelf space. RTDs get nothing. Students drink seltzers — 55% of Gen Z adults 21–24 have already tried hard seltzer. Campus venues haven't caught up. That's the gap SwampWater is built to fill.

We're in pre-launch, building the model at UF first — structured from day one to scale to every SEC campus that's already said yes to hard seltzer and hasn't had a purpose-built brand to put there.

01

Campus & Venue Assessment

We map each campus's venue landscape, existing beverage agreements, regulatory environment, and compliance requirements to identify where SwampWater fits — before a single conversation with a distributor begins.

02

Three-Party Agreement

SwampWater structures the commercial agreement between the campus, the beverage producer, and the distribution partner. One conversation. All three parties aligned. No party has to manage the other two.

03

Campus Activation

We design the on-campus presence — placement, sampling, student-facing marketing, and game-day activation. Built for the university channel specifically, not adapted from a retail or bar playbook.

04

Repeatable Playbook

Everything built at UF — the compliance templates, the distributor relationships, the activation framework — transfers to the next campus. The model gets faster and cheaper with every launch.

Frequently Asked

Common Questions About University Beverage Partnerships

Is the NCAA okay with hard seltzer at campus venues?
Yes. The NCAA officially cleared the sale of hard seltzer and cider at stadium events in March 2023. This was a meaningful policy update that opened the category campus-wide. SwampWater Vodka Seltzer is designed to fit within these guidelines at venues that have approved alcohol sales.
What is the current status of SwampWater?
SwampWater is pre-launch. The product has been formulated and the first batch produced. We are actively building relationships at the University of Florida and structuring the model for SEC expansion. We are not yet commercially active at any venue.
Why does the university RTD market matter right now?
All 16 SEC schools sell alcohol publicly as of 2024. Texas A&M generated $2.4M in gross alcohol revenue in their first year of general sales. 55% of Gen Z adults 21–24 have tried hard seltzer. The demand is there. The policy is there. The dedicated campus RTD brand is not — yet.
How can a university or athletics department work with SwampWater?
If you're at a university venue — stadium, student union, arena, or campus dining — reach out directly. We're building our first partnerships now. We handle brand selection, distributor alignment, and activation. You gain a new revenue category without managing any of it.
How can a beverage brand partner with SwampWater?
We're open to conversations with producers who have Florida distribution capacity and a product that fits the campus venue environment. SwampWater handles the university relationship layer — you provide the product and production capability. Email us directly.
What makes SwampWater different from a regular distributor?
A distributor delivers product. SwampWater builds the university channel from scratch — licensing navigation, campus relationship development, compliance structuring, and student-facing activation. We're not a distributor. We're the infrastructure the distributor plugs into.
Where does SwampWater operate?
Florida first. We're based in Gainesville and actively building at UF. The near-term roadmap is the SEC — 16 schools across the Southeast, all with alcohol sales approved, none with a dedicated RTD campus brand. That's the gap we're filling.
Why start at UF?
Gatorade was invented at UF. The campus that built the sports drink category is the right place to build the campus RTD category. We're not just close to UF — the brand is rooted here. The relationships are here. And Florida is the market that makes the most sense to prove the model before scaling to the rest of the SEC.

But beer wasn't what people in our generation were buying. Seltzers were. RTDs were. The question became obvious: why not build the campus-native RTD brand that doesn't exist yet?
